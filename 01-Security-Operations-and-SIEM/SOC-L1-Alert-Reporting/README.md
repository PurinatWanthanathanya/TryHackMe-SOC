# Lab: SOC L1 Alert Reporting

**Role:** Junior Security Analyst / Tier 1 SOC Analyst

## 🎯 Objective
To master incident documentation and alert reporting methodologies by applying the 5 Ws framework, establishing clear escalation criteria for Tier 2 analysts, and adhering to secure communication protocols during critical security incidents.

## 🚀 Execution Steps
1. **Structured Incident Documentation (5 Ws Framework):** 
   * Compiled comprehensive incident reports utilizing the **5 Ws framework** (`Who`, `What`, `When`, `Where`, and `Why`) to ensure all critical forensic artifacts and timelines were accurately captured.
   * Structured alert documentation to facilitate rapid root-cause analysis and seamless handover to Tier 2 (L2) incident responders without loss of investigative context.
2. **Alert Triage & Escalation Decision-Making:** 
   * Evaluated security alerts against standardized escalation criteria to determine appropriate response paths.
   * Identified scenarios requiring immediate L2 escalation, including high-severity threats, incidents demanding system-level configuration changes, cases requiring external coordination, or complex threats exceeding L1 analytical scope.
3. **Secure Communication & Escalation Protocols:** 
   * Applied out-of-band communication protocols during suspected breaches, avoiding potentially compromised channels (e.g., internal chat or email) in favor of secure alternatives such as direct phone calls.
   * Executed management escalation procedures, initiating administrative handover if Tier 2 responders remained unresponsive for over 30 minutes during high-priority incidents.

## 📊 Results & Evidence
* Successfully documented simulated security incidents using standardized reporting templates, applied appropriate L2 escalation rules, and demonstrated secure out-of-band communication handling.

## 🧠 Key Takeaways
* **The Power of the 5 Ws:** Learned that effective alert reporting relies on clarity and completeness—answering Who, What, When, Where, and Why preserves digital evidence and saves critical time for Tier 2 analysts during deep investigations.
* **Knowing When to Escalate:** Recognized that a Tier 1 analyst's responsibility isn't to solve every complex problem alone, but to accurately triage and promptly escalate severe threats, system-level changes, or ambiguous attacks to L2 specialists.
* **Never Use Compromised Channels:** Understood a crucial incident response rule—if an enterprise network or communication tool is suspected of being compromised, analysts must switch to out-of-band methods (like phone calls) to prevent attackers from intercepting remediation plans.
* **SLA & Accountability:** Concluded that time is critical in incident response; strictly following escalation timers (such as escalating to management after 30 minutes of L2 unresponsiveness) prevents bottlenecks during active breaches.
